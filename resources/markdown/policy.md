This policy explains what Dot.Press stores about you and your work, what it does with it, who can see it and how to have it corrected or deleted. Dot.Press is run by **BluPin Incorporated** ("we", "us") and is part of the Dot ecosystem. It is written with South Africa's **Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA)** in mind.

## 1. Who is responsible

BluPin Incorporated is the responsible party for the personal information in Dot.Press. Questions, requests and complaints go to our Information Officer at privacy@infodot.co.za, the same address InfoDot uses.

## 2. What Dot.Press stores

**Your account.** Your name, your email address and the teams you belong to, with your role in each. If your account has a password, only a salted hash of it is stored, never the password.

**Your decks.** Everything you put in a deck: slide text, notes, charts and their data, tables, themes and brand kits. Each deck keeps restore points (the latest 40 automatic ones, plus any you name) so you can go back.

**Files you upload.** Pictures, video, audio and other files you add to a deck.

**Comments, reviews and approvals.** Comments on slides, review requests and the decisions people record on them, with the name of the person and the time.

**Activity.** A record on each deck of who did what and when, for example "shared", "exported" or "restored a version".

**Changes in flight.** While people edit, each change is written to a short log so editors stay in step. Entries older than 14 days are deleted.

**Presenting.** A presenting session stores which slide is showing, so a phone remote and people following along can keep up. A list of who has a deck open right now is shown to people who can open that deck.

**Exports.** When you export a deck, the finished PowerPoint or PDF file is kept on the server for 24 hours so you can download it, then deleted.

**AI records.** When you ask for an outline or a suggestion, we keep your prompt, a shortened copy of the answer, token counts, timing and whether our safety checks blocked the request. See section 5.

**Technical information.** Your IP address and browser details, tied to your sign-in session, to keep you signed in, to show you your browser sessions and to protect against abuse.

**Backups.** The database is copied once a day and the newest 14 copies are kept.

## 3. Why we use it

- To let you sign in and to keep you signed in.
- To store, edit, share, present, export and restore your decks.
- To show collaborators the comments, reviews and activity that belong to a deck.
- To run the review and the suggestions you ask for.
- To keep Dot.Press secure, to limit abuse and to recover from failures.

We do not sell your information and we do not use it for advertising. Dot.Press has no analytics or tracking scripts.

## 4. Signing in through InfoDot

Dot.Press accounts come from InfoDot. When you open Dot.Press from InfoDot, InfoDot sends a short-lived, single-use token to Dot.Press in a form post, so the token is never in a web address. Dot.Press checks it with InfoDot and signs you in. The first time, this creates your account from the name and email address InfoDot holds. Your password is never sent to Dot.Press by InfoDot.

## 5. Suggestions and AI

The review, the outline builder and the suggestions run as plain rules on our server. Your text is not sent to anyone else for that.

A language model is used only if the server has been set up with a key for one, and then only when you ask for something that needs it. In that case the text you ask about is sent to the model provider to produce the answer: the brief or source text you pasted for an outline, or the text of the slide you asked about. Every answer says whether it came from local rules or from a model. Whether model mode is on depends on how the server is set up, not on anything you can change in your account.

Nothing a model or a rule suggests changes your deck until you accept it.

## 6. Who can see your decks

You choose, for each deck: private (you and the people you name), your team, or anyone with a link.

**Link sharing.** A link contains a 40 character random code. Anyone who has the link can open the deck, so treat it like a key. You can protect a link with a password (stored as a hash) and give it an expiry date. People who open the link see the slides only: no speaker notes, comments, versions or hidden slides. Turning link sharing off, or letting the link expire, makes it answer "not found" and an old link never works again. Shared pages are marked so search engines do not index them, are not cached, and do not pass the link to other sites.

**Presenting links.** The audience link and the phone remote link of a live session work for whoever has them, for up to 12 hours. They stop sooner when you end the session, when the deck is archived, or when the person who started the session loses access to the deck. The audience link shows the slides only, never speaker notes.

**Teams.** People in your team see the decks you share with the team, with the rights their role gives them.

**Comments and activity** are visible to everyone who can open the deck.

## 7. Exports, imports and files you download

Exports and the offline present package are built on the server and handed to you. Once you have the file, it is outside our control: it holds your content in full, so store and share it with care. When you import a PowerPoint file or chart data from a spreadsheet, the file is read on the server and its content becomes part of a deck.

## 8. How long we keep it

We keep your account and decks while your account is active. Deleting a deck deletes its slides, restore points, comments, activity and shared links. Deleting your account deletes the teams you own, with every deck, file and comment inside them. Decks you made in a team you do not own stay with that team and no longer name you. Download anything you want to keep first.

Some copies last a little longer: the daily database backups for up to 14 days, and the change log for up to 14 days. Export files go after 24 hours. We may keep records the law requires us to keep.

## 9. Security

Passwords are stored as hashes. Files are served through signed addresses and every request is checked against your team and role. Pages carry browser protections such as content security headers. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise it.

- ask us to delete it;
